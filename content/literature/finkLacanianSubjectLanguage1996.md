## one {#one}


### language and otherness {#language-and-otherness}


#### a slip of the Other's tongue {#a-slip-of-the-other-s-tongue}

**analysand** - the person engaged in analyzing themselves

two different types of discourse or 'talk':

-   **ego talk** - everyday talk about what we consciously think and believe about
    ourselves
-   _some other kind of talk_

we can assume that there are more kinds of talk, but that they come from two
different psychological places: the ego or self, and the Other.

psychoanalysis assumes that the Other talk stems from a locatable _other_. Freud
called the Other **the unconscious**, and Lacan goes further to state that the
unconscious is the Other's discourse.

| ego/self discourse | Other discourse |
|--------------------|-----------------|
| conscious          | unconscious     |
| intentional        | unintentional   |

most people prone to slips of the tongue believe that this is a random event,
but Freud would say "the truth has spoken".

in "The Agency of the Letter in the Unconscious", Lacan related the Freudian
concept of **displacement** and **condensation** (from _The Interpretation of
Dreams, Jokes and Their Relation to the Unconscious_ and _The Psychopathy of
Everyday Life_) to the linguistic notions of **metonymy** and **metaphor**.

how did this Other discourse get inside of us? Lacan accounts for this as
follows:

we are born into a world of discourse, using words that have been passed down to
us through tradition that we had no part in creating or redefining. this
constitutes the **Other of language** or as Lacan calls it in french _l'Autre du
langage_.

as an example, when a child is born, they are bound to learn the language spoken
by their parents. in order to express their wishes, they must go beyond the
crying stage and say what they want in such a way that is comprehensible to
their caretakers. in doing so, their wants are molded by that process, as the
words they need to use in this expression do not necessarily correspond to their
own particular demands.

Lacan takes this further and says that you cannot say that the child even
_knows_ what they want prior to assimilation of language. responding to a baby's
cries with food every time may transform all discomforts into hunger.

the Other as language is assimilated by most children as they attempt to bridge
the gap from inarticulate need and the articulation of desire in a socially
understandable terms.

**alienation in lauguage** - every human being who learns to speak is alienated
from themselves, as language both allows desire to come into being, but also
makes it to where we can never be satisfied once we get what we thought we
wanted.


#### the unconscious {#the-unconscious}

Lacan: _the self is an other, the ego is an other_

| ego/self discourse        | Other discourse |
|---------------------------|-----------------|
| conscious                 | unconscious     |
| intentional               | unintentional   |
| alienated due to language |                 |

the understanding we have about ourselves may be just as farfetched as other
people's view of us. this breaks down the notion of the self as some sort of
innermost part of a person.

Lacan states that _the unconscious is language_ - language is what makes up the
unconcious.

Freud referred to what is repressed as **ideational representatives**, which Lacan
translates to _representants de la representation_, which can be equated with
the linguistic concept of **signifiers**.

Lacan constantly stresses that _the unconcious is structured like a language_,
by which he did not mean that it is structured exactly like a modern language,
but that it obeys a grammar, a set of rules that govern the transformation.

the unconcious is full of foreign desires, expectations, etc.

internalizations of the Other's discourse can be found in the conscience or what
Freud called the **superego**.


#### foreign bodies {#foreign-bodies}

we can distinguish different subject positions (clinical structures) as
different relations to the Other.
